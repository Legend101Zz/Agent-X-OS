## Pricing structure (3 ways — lead with the ladder)

**1. Free pilot (Attraction offer):** 5 verified prospects + drafted outreach, 72 hrs. Cost to
you: ~nothing. Job: prove quality, kill the "AI junk" objection.

**2. One-time batch (the on-ramp):** **$300–$500 for 20** verified prospects + drafted
outreach + 2 follow-ups each. Job: let them buy *once* with low risk, get a result, build trust.

**3. Monthly retainer (Continuity — the goal):** **$750–$1,500/mo** for ~20/week + drafted
outreach + the bonuses (Reply Playbook, monthly "who's underserved" report). Job: recurring
revenue. *Continuity offers* are where the real money is (Money Models, Section V).

**Optional — Pay-for-performance (Upsell for the brave):** charge **per booked call** or **per
closed client** instead of per batch. Highest trust, highest fear-removal for them (it's the
22.4× Grand Slam from file 01). Use once your delivery is proven.

### Why not "per lead"?
Per-lead pricing re-commoditizes you (back to comparing you to junk-list vendors on price)
and caps your upside. Price the **outcome + the done-for-you outreach**, not the row count.

## Guarantees that remove risk for BOTH sides
(*$100M Offers* Ch. 15 logic, tuned so you're never the one holding the bag)

- **Their risk → gone:** "First 5 free; on paid batches, if <15 of 20 are genuine fits, you
  don't pay / I redo it." (Conditional guarantee — only on quality *you* control.)
- **Your risk → managed:** you only guarantee **fit quality**, *not* that they'll close deals
  (you don't control their sales skill). Never guarantee "X clients" on the retainer — that's
  outside your control. (Pay-for-performance is the *only* place you take outcome risk, and
  only by choice.)

## Getting paid as an Indian founder from US/UK/EU clients

Based on 2026 comparisons (**verify current fees/eligibility before you rely on these**):

| Method | Best for | Rough cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wise (Business)** | Your **default** | ~0.5–2% | Get local US/UK/EU receiving account details; mid-market FX, lowest total fees |
| **Payoneer** | Backup / marketplaces | low–moderate | US/UK/EU virtual accounts; good if a client insists on it |
| **Stripe** | If you build a checkout page | ~3–5% | Cleanest client UX for cards, **but** verify Stripe's availability/requirements for an India-based entity (often needs Stripe Atlas / a US entity — confirm before promising) |
| **PayPal** | Last resort / client convenience | ~6.5–8.4% | Easy for clients, expensive for you; avoid for big invoices |

**Recommended setup:** **Wise Business as primary** (open it now — verification takes days),
**Payoneer as backup**, and only add **Stripe** if/when you want a card checkout for the
retainer. Send a simple invoice (Wise can generate one) with clear terms.

**Things to verify yourself (don't take my word):**
- Whether you need to register as an exporter / get an **IEC code** for service exports from India.
- **GST on export of services** treatment (often zero-rated with LUT — confirm with a CA).
- **FIRC / FIRA** (foreign inward remittance proof) — keep these for every payment for tax.
- Whether a US LLC (e.g., via Stripe Atlas) is worth it later for trust + Stripe access.

> **Reality check (your critical friend):** the payment plumbing is a real, solvable friction —
> not a blocker. Set up Wise this week so a "yes" never stalls on "how do I pay you." But this
> is also where a quick call with a CA who does export-of-services is worth every rupee — get
> the GST/FIRC treatment right from invoice #1.

## Reality check (your critical friend)
- **Don't underprice to feel safe.** A $50 price signals junk and attracts your worst clients
  (Money Models / Offers Ch. 5). Use the *free → starter → retainer* ladder to earn the price,
  not a permanent discount.
- **Get one testimonial fast, then raise prices.** Your first 1–2 clients can be on the starter
  price in exchange for a written result you can quote. After that, charge the retainer.
- **Recurring > one-time.** Push toward the monthly retainer; that's the model that compounds.
