# 04 — Objection Handling (word-for-word)

> Method: Hormozi's **ACA — Acknowledge, Compare, Ask** (used throughout *$100M Leads*
> /*Offers*). Acknowledge their point so they feel understood ("the point of good persuasion
> is for the prospect to feel **understood**" — *$100M Offers*, Ch. 4), reframe with a
> comparison, then ask one question to move forward. Read these out loud.

**Golden rule:** when you freeze, fall back to one line — *"Totally fair. Can I just send the 5
free so you can see for yourself?"* The free sample resolves most objections by showing.

---

## 1. "We already do our own marketing / get our own leads."
> "Makes sense — most firms I talk to do *some* of their own. *[Acknowledge]* The thing is,
> the firms doing their own usually tell me the bottleneck isn't *ideas*, it's **time to find and
> write to the right people each week.** *[Compare]* That's the exact part I do for you — and
> the first 5 are free. *[Ask]* If I send 5 this week, would you actually look at them?"

## 2. "AI is sketchy — how do I know these are real / any good?"  *(the big one)*
This is the #1 objection in the market, and you have the best possible answer. The research
is on your side: buyers got burned because "AI-sourced leads looked great on paper but
response rates were abysmal," 30%+ of bought lists are junk, and vendors resell the same
contact to ten companies. **Your tool is the opposite of that.**
> "You *should* be skeptical — honestly, most 'AI leads' are recycled junk lists sold to ten
> people at once, and they don't work. *[Acknowledge]* Mine are the opposite: every prospect
> comes with **the actual quote from their own website** that shows why they fit, and a **real
> contact path** — not a guessed email. *[Compare]* That's why I send the **first 5 free** —
> don't trust me, **check them yourself.** *[Ask]* Want me to send the 5 so you can verify them?"

## 3. "It's too expensive."
> "Fair — let's make sure it's worth it before you spend anything. *[Acknowledge]* One new
> [client/engagement] is worth what to you over a year — a few thousand, more? *[Compare]*
> The batch costs less than [a fraction of one client], and you saw the first 5 free, so you're
> not betting blind. *[Ask]* If even one of these 20 becomes a client, does the math work?"

*(Never lower the price to "rescue" the sale — Hormozi, Ch. 5: lowering price is "a one-way
road to destruction." If they truly can't afford the retainer, offer the one-time starter batch,
not a discount on the retainer.)*

## 4. "Just send me some info." *(the polite brush-off)*
> "I can do better than info — *[Acknowledge]* info is just me talking about myself. *[Compare]*
> Let me send the actual **5 free prospects** instead; that shows you exactly what you'd get,
> with real names you can check. *[Ask]* What's the best email for them?"

## 5. "I need to think about it." / "Let me check with my partner."
> "Of course — this should be an easy yes only *after* you've seen the quality. *[Acknowledge]*
> So let's lower the stakes: I'll send the **5 free** now, you and [partner] look at real names
> on your own time, no commitment. *[Compare]* Then 'thinking about it' is thinking about
> something real, not a pitch. *[Ask]* Should I send them to just you, or you and [partner]?"

## 6. "We're too busy right now."
> "That's exactly the firms I'm built for — *[Acknowledge]* being too busy to prospect is the
> whole problem I solve. *[Compare]* You don't lift a finger: I find them, verify them, write the
> first email; you just approve. The free 5 take you 90 seconds to skim. *[Ask]* Can I send
> them and you glance when you have a coffee?"

## 7. "Why is an Indian founder emailing me?" *(unspoken, but real — preempt trust)*
You won't always hear this, but assume it's in their head. Don't hide; lead with proof and let
the work speak.
> "Fair question — I'm a solo founder in India, and I built this tool myself. *[Acknowledge]*
> That's actually *why* the first 5 are free and why everything comes with verifiable proof —
> I'd rather you check real names than take my word. *[Compare]* You're not paying for trust;
> you're paying only after you've seen it work. *[Ask]* Want the 5 so you can judge for
> yourself?"

---

## The one move under all of these
Every objection routes to the same low-risk yes: **"Can I send you the 5 free so you can see
for yourself?"** You almost never have to "win" an argument — you let the free sample do it.
That's the Grand Slam logic: make the next step so safe that *no* is the unreasonable answer.

## Reality check (your critical friend)
- **Don't argue. Acknowledge first, always.** A defensive founder loses; a calm "you should
  be skeptical" wins.
- **The AI-skepticism objection is your friend.** It lets you *contrast* with junk vendors and
  point at your proof. Welcome it.
- **If they say a hard no, thank them and move on.** Rejection = data, not a verdict on you
  (file 06). Next.
