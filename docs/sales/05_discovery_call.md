# 05 — The Discovery Call (beginner-proof, read-from-screen)

> You freeze on calls — so this is written to be **read aloud**. The goal of the call is NOT to
> close hard; it's to let them **sell themselves** by talking about their problem, then hand
> them the free sample. Based on Hormozi's "the pain is the pitch" (*$100M Offers*, Ch. 4) and
> the Value-Equation questions (Ch. 6).

**Mindset for the call:** you are a *helpful expert sending a free gift*, not a salesman
begging. You already have something valuable to give (the 5). That's your spine.

---

## The shape of the call (15–20 min)

1. **Open & disarm** (30 sec)
2. **5–7 questions that make them sell themselves** (8–10 min — *they talk 80%*)
3. **Mirror the pain back** (1 min)
4. **Present the offer** (2 min)
5. **Ask for the next step** (1 min)
6. **Handle "let me think / check with partner"** (as needed)

---

## 1. Open & disarm (read verbatim)
> "Hey [Name], thanks for the few minutes. I'll keep it short and useful. My plan today is
> just to understand how you get new clients now, and if it makes sense, I'll send you 5 real,
> best-fit prospects for free so you can see what I do. Sound okay? *[wait for yes]* Cool —
> mind if I ask you a few quick questions first?"

## 2. The 5–7 questions (let them talk — this is where they sell themselves)
Ask, then **shut up and listen.** Jot notes. Each maps to a Value-Equation lever.

1. "How do you get most of your new clients today — referrals, repeat, marketing?"
2. "When you *do* want more clients, what do you actually do to find them?" *(finds the pain)*
3. "What's the hardest or most annoying part of that?" *(this is "the pain is the pitch")*
4. "If you had 10 more good-fit clients this year, what would that mean for the firm?" *(Dream Outcome — let them say it)*
5. "Have you tried buying leads or lists before? How did that go?" *(usually surfaces the 'junk list' pain → your wedge)*
6. "What does a good client look like for you — size, industry, the ones you wish you had more of?" *(this is your ICP input)*
7. "If finding those was handled for you, what would you do with the time back?" *(reduces their Effort & Sacrifice in their own words)*

## 3. Mirror the pain back (so they feel understood)
> "So if I've got it right: you get clients mostly from [X], but when you want *more* you have
> to [the annoying thing they said], you've been burned by [junk lists / no time], and a
> handful of good-fit clients would mean [their dream answer]. Did I miss anything?"
*(Almost always: "No, that's exactly it." Now they've sold themselves.)*

## 4. Present the offer (short — tie each line to what they said)
> "Here's what I do, and it lines up with exactly that. Every week I hand you 20 real, named
> businesses that match the good-client profile you just described — each with proof from
> their own website that they fit, a real contact path, and the first email written for you to
> approve. You don't prospect, you don't write, you just say 'send.' And because you've been
> burned before, the **first 5 are free, in 72 hours** — you check them yourself before paying
> anything."

## 5. Ask for the close (one clear ask — then be quiet)
> "So here's what I'd suggest: let me put together your free 5 this week based on the profile
> you described. If they're as good as I think, we line up a full batch of 20. **Want me to
> start on the 5?**"
*(Then STOP TALKING. Silence is fine. Let them answer.)*

## 6. "Let me think about it" / "I need to check with my partner"
> "Totally reasonable — and the free 5 makes that easy. Let me send them so you and
> [partner] are deciding on **real names you can verify**, not on my pitch. Want them sent to
> just you, or both of you? And when's good to circle back — this Friday or early next week?"
*(You get the sample in their hands AND a specific follow-up time. Never leave without a date.)*

## Reality check (your critical friend)
- **Talking less wins.** Beginners over-explain. Ask, then *count to three* before you fill
  silence. Their words are your best sales copy.
- **Don't discount on the call.** If price stalls, drop to the one-time starter batch, never a
  cheaper retainer.
- **Always leave with a next action + date.** "I'll send the 5 today; let's talk Friday." Vague
  endings = dead leads.
