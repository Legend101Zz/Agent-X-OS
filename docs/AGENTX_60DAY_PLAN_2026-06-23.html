02 The crux — three reframes that decide the quarter

If we get these three right, the plan writes itself.

01

The bottleneck is distribution, not mandates.

We are technical founders — building is our comfort zone, and comfort is the trap. With eight weeks, every hour spent building a mandate nobody has agreed to pay for is an hour stolen from finding the person who will. Treat paying customers as the scarcest resource in the company, and ruthlessly protect the hours that hunt for them.

02

Selling is improving the OS.

Our own Blueprint says it: the OS gets good because real runs settle against reality and fill the gym. So shipping one mandate to one paying customer improves the core product more than a month of solo coding — corrections become rules, the heap fills, the moat compounds.

That means "improve the OS" is not a third workstream competing for time. It's the exhaust of pillars 1 and 2. The fastest path to a better OS runs straight through a customer's invoice.

BLUEPRINT.md §7 — "get one instance to settle() against reality ~100 times… the OS story stops being a diagram and becomes a balance sheet."
03

No mandate #3 until #1 or #2 has a paying customer.

Discovery is seductive — there is always a shinier pain on Reddit to chase. Resist it. One sold mandate beats three half-built ones. Mandate-discovery keeps running in the background to tee up the next pick, but we do not invest build hours in a third mandate until money has changed hands on one we already have.

$100M Offers, Ch. 4 — "Pick one. No one can serve two masters… Don't make me niche slap you."

How we do volume (without torching the domain)

  • Separate sending domain + SPF/DKIM/DMARC; warm it 4–6 weeks before scale.
  • ≤ 10–15 sends/day while warming. Narrow lists (<200) get ~2× reply, ~4.4× positive.
  • Automate sending + follow-up reminders through the OS scheduler. Human approves; the machine remembers.
  • Wise Business set up now so a "yes" never stalls on "how do I pay you."

Where the human is non-negotiable

  • Calls stay human — scripted, read-aloud, and only after a warm reply. Don't automate them.
  • One line of personal context on every drafted email before it sends (the known G4 gap).
  • Approve every send — this is the L1 trust rung, and it's also our quality gate.
  • Track sent, not replies — the one metric we control. Reps over outcomes (file 06).

07 Decision gates — when to stop

The exact line where we stop polishing and pivot.

You asked for this specifically: when do we not keep trying on a mandate? The trap for first-time founders is the slow death of polishing a product nobody bought. Here are the tripwires — numeric, unemotional, decided in advance.

First, the reframe

On the first "no", don't kill the product — kill the ICP and the message. Hormozi: "If you try one hundred offers, I promise you will succeed." A dead ICP is data that points discovery at the next one. Only after you've exhausted a reasonable set of ICPs do you question the mandate itself.

Tripwire 1 · the ICP

Switch the ICP, not the product

50 personalized touches · full 5–7 follow-up · ≥ 3 conversations → 0 free-pilot acceptances
Then: that ICP is dead. Change the target and message — the product is fine.
Tripwire 2 · the mandate

Shelve the mandate

3 dead ICPs · ~150 touches · ~8–10 conversations → 0 paid pilots
Then: demand is unproven. Stop building. Reallocate to the lane with pull. Don't polish.
Tripwire 3 · the CA bet (pre-build)

Pause before adding features

~10 warm CA contacts · pilot offered → 0 say "I'd pay $X" / take a deposit (within 2 wks)
Then: pause the CA build. Don't add features to an unsold tool. Demand first, code second.
Tripwire 4 · the weekly guard

The sunk-cost check, every Friday

Ask: did this week produce a new conversation or a payment? → "No" twice in a row
Then: change the input (list / offer / channel) — not the effort. Doing more of a dead thing isn't grit.

One rule under all four: we pivot, we don't quit the company. The OS, the playbook, and both mandates' machinery survive every pivot — a dead ICP or mandate just retargets discovery at the next, better-evidenced pick.

10 What we are not doing

The discipline is mostly in the no's.

✕
No mandate #3 pre-revenue. Discovery validates; it doesn't get build hours until #1 or #2 pays.
✕
No Foundry / compiler / marketplace build now. Those are post-customer. The gym fills from real runs first.
✕
No WhatsApp, voice, payments-as-agent, or browser-as-default. Out of scope this quarter.
✕
No underpricing. A $50 price signals junk and attracts the worst clients. Earn the price via the free→starter→retainer ladder.
✕
No mass-blasting from the main domain. Our whole brand is "real and verified." One spammy blast torches it for months.
✕
No perfecting the product instead of selling it. The machine runs. Polishing is procrastination with a clean conscience.
Every Friday, one question:
did this week produce a conversation or a payment?

Everything in this plan exists to make the honest answer "yes" more often than "no." Build less. Send more. Let reality compile the rest.
